PLAN: INDEPENDENT MULTI-OBJECTIVE OPTIMIZATION OF THE THREE BEST MODELS
Status: planning only. Nothing has been implemented. Implementation starts after the
current work has been pushed, unchanged, to the GitHub repository.
Date: 2026-11-19


1. WHAT THE PROFESSORS ARE ASKING FOR

So far we did the following: we took the paper's optimal designs (Table 5: PV number, TES
capacity, SOEC power, DF, TES factor), kept them fixed, and searched over model settings
(TES limits, PV model, discount rate, LCOE basis and so on) until the model's DPSP, EWP and
LCOE came close to the paper's values. That shows the model can reproduce the paper's
numbers at the paper's designs. It does not show that the model would have found those
designs.

Now they want the reverse:
  - Keep each of the three best models fixed (model settings frozen).
  - Let a multi-objective optimizer choose the decision variables itself.
  - Check whether the optimizer arrives at (or near) the paper's optimal solutions.

If an independent optimization of our model lands near the paper's Table 5 solutions and
shows the same hourly behaviour as Figs. 8-10, then the model is good enough to use as the
environment for reinforcement learning.

The three models (Approach 2, per mode, from results/best_per_mode_final_20261111):
  Mode 1 model: TM-P0521-F157
    TES charge/discharge 10/15 MWth absolute, PV 150 W nameplate, reactor 25 MWth,
    keep_split, start; real rate 10%, 20 years, electric variable cost basis,
    requested load, BOP embedded, retirement decommissioning.
  Mode 2 model: TM-P0523-F158
    TES 10/15 MWth absolute, PV 150 W, 25 MWth, renewables_first;
    10%, 20 years, electric, requested load, BOP embedded, project decommissioning.
  Mode 3 model: TM-P0441-F115
    TES 10/7.5 MWth both_scaled (scaled by TES capacity / 2000), PV 150 W, 25 MWth,
    keep_split, start; 8%, 30 years, thermal, served load, separate BOP proxy,
    retirement decommissioning.
Each model is optimized only in its own mode (Mode 1 model in Mode 1, and so on).
Excel inputs stay unchanged; the teacher's files stay unchanged (hash-checked).


2. WHAT FIGS. 8, 9 AND 10 ARE IN THE PAPER (2025 paper, NED 440, 114138)

All three are hourly time series over one year (0 to 8760 h) of one specific optimal
solution: the solution in Table 5 with the lowest EWP among Pareto solutions with DPSP < 1%.

  Fig. 8 (Mode 1, Table 5 row: PV 60,423, TES 2000 MWh_th):
    panels: LF of TES, DPS, WEN. (No hydrogen and no WER, because Mode 1 has no SOEC
    and renewable power is always below demand.)
  Fig. 9 (Mode 2, PV 80,000, TES 2000, SOEC 9.0870 MWe, DF 0.5843):
    panels: produced hydrogen, LF of TES, DPS, WEN, WER.
  Fig. 10 (Mode 3, PV 75,054, TES 250, SOEC 9.5445, DF 0.3245, TES factor 0.8048):
    same five panels. This is the lowest-EWP Mode 3 solution with the extra conditions
    DPSP < 1% and LCOE < 0.0940 (the Mode 1 LCOE), i.e. the last row of Table 5.

In the teacher's script these correspond to his plotting outputs Figure2_Mode1,
Figure3_Mode2 and Figure4_Mode3, which we have already been comparing with the paper.

Qualitative features the paper's text (Section 3.2.2) states, to use as a checklist:
  Mode 1 (Fig. 8)
    - TES LF shows seasonal behaviour on top of daily cycling.
    - Energy accumulates in TES before about 1500 h and is consumed after about 7000 h.
    - TES empties frequently after about 7800 h, so DPS appears in that period.
    - WEN occurs when TES is full, concentrated in about 1400-4000 h and 4900-7000 h.
    - No WER.
    - LF minimum around 4450 h is about 0.71.
  Mode 2 (Fig. 9)
    - Seasonal TES behaviour, similar to Mode 1.
    - LF drops to about 0.88 at about 4450 h (higher than 0.71 in Mode 1).
    - In 7800-8760 h hydrogen production continues, TES drains, DPS appears.
    - WEN clearly lower than in Mode 1.
    - WER appears when the renewable power sent to the SOEC is below its operating power.
  Mode 3 (Fig. 10)
    - Hydrogen production is shut down most of the time after about 7500 h.
    - LF drops to about 0.088 at about 4450 h (small 250 MWh TES).
    - DPS concentrated in 7500-8760 h.
    - WEN clearly lower than in Modes 1 and 2.
Known gaps of our models (already seen in the grid-search plots): Mode 2 model gives
WER = 0 (paper shows WER), and no model reproduces the Mode 3 LF dip near 4450 h.
These should be reported again after the optimization, not hidden.

Other parts of the paper that the comparison should use:
  - Table 2: decision variable bounds.
  - Table A4: algorithm settings.
  - Fig. 6: hypervolume convergence over iterations (20 independent runs).
  - Fig. 7: 3-D Pareto fronts (DPSP, EWP, LCOE) per mode, WT number = 1.
  - Table 3: CRITIC weights. Table 4: CRITIC-TOPSIS compromise solutions.
  - Table 5: lowest-EWP solutions with DPSP < 1% (the main reference).


3. THE OPTIMIZATION PROBLEM, AS THE PAPER DEFINES IT

Objectives (all minimized, all three modes): DPSP, EWP, LCOE.
WT number fixed at 1 (the paper treats WT number as a scenario, not a variable).

Decision variables and bounds (Table 2):
  PV number      40,000 to 80,000 (integer)          Modes 1, 2, 3
  TES capacity   250 to 2000 MWh_th                   Modes 1, 2, 3
  SOEC power     0.01 x P_max_renewable to P_max_renewable (MWe)   Modes 2, 3
  Demand factor  0.2 to 1                             Modes 2, 3
  TES factor     0.2 to 1                             Mode 3 only
So Mode 1 has 2 variables, Mode 2 has 4, Mode 3 has 5.

P_max_renewable depends on the PV number (it is the peak of PV + wind output), so the SOEC
bound moves with PV. Plan: optimize a fraction s in [0.01, 1] and set
SOEC = s x P_max_renewable(PV). This keeps the bound exact without constraint handling.

Hydrogen tank size is not a variable: it is set from the maximum daily hydrogen mass, as in
the paper and the teacher's script.

DPSP < 1% is not a constraint during the optimization in the paper. It is a filter applied
afterwards to the Pareto set. We do the same.

Algorithm settings from Table A4:
  MOPSO: population 500, 100 iterations, inertia weight 0.4, c1 = 1, c2 = 2.
  (NSGA-II / NSGA-III: population 500, 100 iterations, crossover 0.6, SBX index 20,
   1 mutated variable expected, polynomial mutation index 20.)
The paper ran everything in PlatEMO (MATLAB) and chose MOPSO.


4. HOW WE WOULD DO IT

Step 0. Push the current work to GitHub unchanged (you will send the link). All new work
        goes into a new folder (e.g. reproducibility_search/independent_optimization/) and
        new results folders. Existing files and results are not modified.

Step 1. Evaluation function per model.
        A function f(model, mode, x) -> (DPSP, EWP, LCOE) that runs the patched teacher
        engine with the frozen model settings and the decision vector x.
        Check before anything else: at the paper's Table 5 designs, f must return exactly
        the values already stored in best_per_mode_final_20261111 (bit-for-bit). Also check
        the teacher files' SHA-256 before and after.

Step 2. Measure the cost of one evaluation.
        One optimizer run = 500 x 100 = 50,000 simulations per mode. The paper repeated
        each algorithm 20 times. We time one simulation from a fresh process and multiply.
        If it is too slow, two options:
          a) Parallelize over the 11 cores (each particle is independent).
          b) Write a fast (numba) copy of the hourly dispatch, accepted only if it matches
             the patched teacher engine to within 1e-12 on a few hundred random designs and
             exactly at the Table 5 designs.
        Decide the number of repeated runs (target 20 as in the paper, minimum 5) from the
        measured time.

Step 3. Optimizer.
        Main: MOPSO with the Table A4 settings, following Coello and Lechuga (2002) as
        implemented in PlatEMO (external repository of non-dominated solutions, grid-based
        leader selection, mutation, archive size = 500). pymoo (already installed) has no
        MOPSO, so this would be our own implementation, tested on standard benchmark
        problems (e.g. ZDT/DTLZ) against known fronts before being used on the NRHES.
        Cross-check: NSGA-II and NSGA-III from pymoo with the Table A4 settings. If the
        different algorithms give the same front, the result does not depend on the
        algorithm.
        PV number rounded to integer; fixed random seeds; every run logged.

Step 4. Convergence.
        Hypervolume per iteration, mean and standard deviation over the repeated runs
        (our version of Fig. 6). The fronts of all runs merged into one final Pareto set
        per mode.

Step 5. Select solutions exactly the way the paper did.
        a) Table 5 analogue: from our Pareto set, solutions with DPSP < 1%, sorted by EWP;
           report the lowest-EWP one and the next two (the paper lists three per mode).
        b) Mode 3 extra row: lowest EWP with DPSP < 1% and LCOE < 0.0940. Also report it
           with the threshold set to our own Mode 1 lowest-EWP LCOE, since the paper's
           rule refers to Mode 1.
        c) Table 3/4 analogue: CRITIC weights from our Pareto set, then TOPSIS compromise
           solution (method from Nie et al. 2024, the 2024 paper).
        d) Fig. 7 analogue: 3-D Pareto front plus the DPSP-EWP and DPSP-LCOE projections.

Step 6. Compare with the paper "in everything".
        i) Objective values: our Table 5 / Table 4 rows against the paper's, as relative
           error and as percentage-point difference (both, since the error definition is
           still open).
        ii) Decision variables: our values against Table 5, as relative difference and as a
           fraction of the Table 2 range (a 2,000-panel difference is 5% of the range).
           Also report whether variables sit on bounds as in the paper (Mode 1 TES = 2000,
           Mode 2 PV = 80,000 and TES = 2000).
        iii) Also place the paper's Table 5 design on our Pareto front: is it dominated by
           our solutions, and by how much? This tells whether a difference in variables
           comes from the model or from the optimizer.
        iv) Time behaviour: run the selected solution through the teacher's plotting
           function and put it next to Figs. 8, 9, 10 (hydrogen, LF, DPS, WEN, WER). Score
           the checklist in section 2 with simple measured quantities: hour and value of
           the LF minimum near 4450 h, share of DPS in 7500/7800-8760 h, WEN in 1400-4000 h
           and 4900-7000 h, share of hours with hydrogen off after 7500 h, total WER,
           hydrogen per year.

Step 7. Report and slides.
        One table per mode (objectives, variables, paper values, differences), Pareto
        plots, convergence plot, side-by-side time series, list of matches and mismatches,
        and a clear answer: does the independent optimum agree with the paper closely
        enough to move on to reinforcement learning.


5. THINGS TO BE AWARE OF

- Circularity: the three models were chosen because they match the paper at the paper's
  designs. That makes it more likely, not certain, that the optimizer finds the same
  designs. If it finds different designs, that is an important result, not a failure of
  the procedure.
- The three models have different financial settings (10% / 20 y, 10% / 20 y, 8% / 30 y),
  so LCOE across modes is not compared like-for-like. The professors asked per model, so
  this is fine, but it should be stated. In particular, the Mode 3 condition
  LCOE < 0.0940 mixes the Mode 3 model with the paper's Mode 1 LCOE.
- Mode 3 TES limits scale with TES capacity (TES/2000) in the Mode 3 model. That rule
  carries over to any TES value the optimizer picks, but it is an assumption of that model
  and the optimizer will exploit it. Modes 1 and 2 use absolute limits.
- Table 5 solutions sit on or near the DPSP = 1% filter. Small model differences can move
  a solution across 1%, which changes which solution is "lowest EWP". Report how sensitive
  the selected solution is to the 1% threshold (e.g. 0.9% and 1.1%).
- Mode 1 Pareto fronts with 2 variables are small; Mode 3 with 5 variables needs the full
  budget. Expect Mode 3 to be the hardest and slowest.
- Disk is about 93% full (about 34 GB free). Store only archives, fronts and the time
  series of the selected solutions, not every simulation.
